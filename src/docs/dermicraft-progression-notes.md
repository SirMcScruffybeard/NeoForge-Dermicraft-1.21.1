# Dermicraft Progression System — Overhaul Notes

Running log for the progression-system overhaul: stretching the Module system's flexibility (see `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md` → "Modules — direction note") past hazard tolerance and past Eater, to a consistent mod-wide framework for how the player unlocks/customizes capability. Companion to `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`, `dermicraft-suit-notes.md`, `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`, and `dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`.

**Why this doc exists, separate from the others:** every other progression-adjacent doc describes its own consumer's numbers. This one is the cross-cutting pass — the taxonomy that decides *which mechanism* a given capability should use, audited against everything that already exists, so future capabilities (and reworks of existing ones) have a rule to check against instead of an ad hoc call each time.

---

## The three mechanism shapes (proposed framework, not yet ratified)

Everything in the mod that gates or grows player/gadget/suit/machine capability already reduces to one of three shapes. None of these are new — each already exists somewhere in the codebase or design corpus — the overhaul is naming them explicitly and auditing every consumer against the set, per the "build the taxonomy first" starting point already logged in the Modules direction note.

### 1. Tier ladder
Permanent, sequential, forced or semi-forced progression. Advancing consumes the previous tier's item/recipe cost; there's no "undo." Grants **capacity and access**, not fine-grained choice.

- Code: `HazardProfile.TIER_1..TIER_4`, `MachineTier`, `NodeTier`, `TieredFloor`, gadget tiers (Eater/Drinker/Sprayer), suit Mark/Type/Series tiers.
- Mechanism: forced-evolution (Evolution Catalyst injection, dumb machines) or willing/button-press evolution (smart structures) or Growth Chamber (suits/gadgets, station not yet built — `dermicraft-project-primer.md` lines 159-174).
- What it answers: "What is this consumer *permanently* capable of right now."

### 2. Points
Permanent currency, spent once, never reclaimed. Buys either a smooth incremental stat rank or a discrete ability unlock. Each consumer (a suit, a gadget) holds its **own separate pool** — shared mechanic, not a shared currency (`dermicraft-gadget-notes.md` line 37).

- Code: none yet — design-only for Eater's material categories, M.A.W.S.'s features; suits' own point-spend menu at the Dock is further along but numbers aren't locked either.
- Earned via: fixed grant per tier-up, plus an escalating-cost buy-in once at max tier (same rule for suits and gadgets, not invented twice).
- What it answers: "How much / which permanent abilities has the player chosen to buy."

### 3. Modules
Destructible, physical, slot-limited items. Field-swappable (Scrench for gadgets; the suit's own swap path). Can **locally exceed** what tier/points alone would currently allow, and — where that's their purpose — are **self-resolving**: the same physical item gets consumed as a tier-up ingredient once the permanent tier catches up to what it was covering.

- Code: `MODULE_DATA`/`SAFETY_MODULE_PROPERTIES` on `EaterItem` only. Kind identified by item tag (`module/safety_heat`, `module/ore`, etc.), not a Java enum, so third-party items can join a category by datapack tag alone.
- **Resolved (2026-08-19):** the suit Add-on → "Module" rename is naming-only. Suit Modules (formerly Add-ons) keep their exact current behavior — permanent equipment, chosen from a roster, docked in the Add-on Frame, stays equipped until the player unequips it. They do **not** become destructible or self-resolving. Only the gadget-side Safety Modules/mouthpieces (and anything designed after them with the same destructible-and-self-resolving shape) carry that behavior.
- **Residual naming note:** "Module" is now a shared label over two genuinely different behavioral patterns — destructible/self-resolving (gadget Safety Modules, mouthpieces, Fluid Bypass) vs. permanent/equipped (suit Modules, ex-Add-ons). This is intentional (both are "an item in a scarce slot that defines a capability"), but it means the word alone doesn't tell you which behavior applies — always check the specific consumer's own doc rather than assuming destructibility (or permanence) from the name.
- What it answers: "What can this consumer do *right now, locally*, beyond its permanent tier/points state" — for the destructible sub-shape specifically, at the cost of the item's own survival; for the suit sub-shape, at the cost of a scarce slot only.

## Audit — existing/designed systems against the taxonomy

| System | Current shape | Taxonomy fit | Notes |

| Machine hazard access (Masticator, Skin Tank, Drooling Cauldron, Effluencer, FL Floor) | Tier ladder only | **Tier ladder** — correct fit, no change needed | Already follows the exact cumulative rule as everything else (`dermicraft-machine-notes.md` lines 133, 413-421: "each tier handles its own hazard class and all below it"). **The gap isn't the tier system — it's that machines have no Module-equivalent slot at all.** **Resolved (2026-08-19):** the gap closes via a dedicated Module tab on an opted-in machine's own screen, not a base `MachineBaseBlockEntity` addition — see Decision Point #2. |
| Eater's material categories (Aggregate, Plant, Animal, Fireball catching) | Points (designed, not built) | **Points** — correct fit | Smooth/discrete permanent unlocks, "rule of fun over strict logical progression" — explicitly not tier-gated or sequenced. No hazard-flexibility angle at all; stays Points. |
| Eater's Safety Modules / mouthpieces | Modules (designed, not built) | **Modules** — correct fit, template case | The one place all three shapes' interaction is actually worked out: Safety Module = local hazard-tier exception, self-resolving into a Growth Chamber ingredient. |
| Eater's Fluid Bypass | Modules (mundane, non-hazard) | **Modules**, but worth flagging | Confirms Modules aren't only a hazard-flexibility mechanism — a Module can just be "a cheap toggle for a targeting rule." Relevant if a future audit tries to define Modules as "the hazard-flex slot" too narrowly. |
| Drinker's hazard restriction | Hardcoded `HazardProfile.TIER_1` constant, no tier/points/module axis at all | **Should be Modules** (named intent, not built) | `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md` line 77 already confirms Drinker should accept the same Safety Module items as Eater. Currently the single clearest "designed but not wired" gap. |
| Sunder chain material / Shatter head | Swappable physical part, own datamap-driven stats (`SUNDER_CHAIN_PROPERTIES`/`SHATTER_HEAD_PROPERTIES`), plus a separate `PART_UPGRADE_TIER` Mutator-upgrade ladder | **Physical part, not Modules** | Not destructible in the Module sense (an equipped chain/head doesn't get "lost," it's a durability object with its own upgrade path). Correctly stays outside all three shapes above — it's a fourth, narrower pattern ("swappable identity part with its own tier"), scoped to exactly two consumers. No reason to force it into Points or Modules. |
| Suit Modules, formerly Add-ons (Bio Vision Goggles, Red/White Muscle, Achilles/Quad Graft, Grapple Module, etc.) | Physical equipment, docked into a universal Module Frame slot, slots grow with suit tier, magnitude tuned via Points | **Rename-only Module, not a Module in the destructible sense** — hybrid of Tier ladder (slots) + Points (magnitude) + a socketed-equipment pick that behaves like Sunder/Shatter's swappable part | **Resolved (2026-08-19):** naming-only rename, no behavioral change — see the residual naming note under "3. Modules" above. Suit Modules stay permanent/equipped, not destructible, despite sharing the "Module" word with Eater's destructible Safety Modules. The Monster Hunter "decoration socket" framing (`dermicraft-suit-notes.md` line 163) still matches Modules' "scarce slot, swappable" spirit — it just doesn't carry destructibility along with it. |

Worth a rename pass in this overhaul since "stretching hazard tolerance mod-wide" is exactly the moment two same-named-but-different "hazard" systems will get confused if left alone. |
| Metaphysical hazard's Mind Rule (machines) | A hazard-tag carve-out (Metaphysical bypasses `HazardProfile` for "dumb" machines/ducts entirely) | **Modifies Tier ladder's scope, not a new shape** | Doesn't need its own taxonomy slot — it's a rule about which hazards a Tier ladder even applies to for a given consumer class, orthogonal to Points/Modules. |

## Decision Points

1. **✅ Resolved (2026-08-19) — Suit Add-ons → Modules is rename-only.** "Add-on" becomes "Module" mod-wide in naming (suit terminology, the Add-on Frame → Module Frame, etc.), with zero behavioral change: suit Modules stay permanent equipment, chosen and kept, never destructible or self-resolving. Only the gadget-side Safety Modules/mouthpieces (and future designs sharing their purpose) carry the destructible/self-resolving behavior. Actual rename pass (code identifiers, item names, lang keys, this doc's own terminology, `dermicraft-suit-notes.md`) is a separate follow-up task, not done as part of this planning pass.
2. **✅ Resolved (2026-08-19) — machine Module slots live in their own screen tab, opt-in per family.** Confirms the opt-in direction already suggested here: a machine only gets Module slots if its screen has a Module tab, and building that tab is a per-machine-family choice — the farming family (hazard-free by design, `dermicraft-machine-notes.md` line 883) simply never gets one, no base-class flag or "off" switch needed.
   - **UI precedent to reuse, not invent:** the Workbench already has exactly this shape — `WorkbenchScreen`'s `Page` enum (`MOD`, `FABRICATION`) plus a vertically-stacked strip of `OPEN_TAB_TEXTURE`/`CLOSED_TAB_TEXTURE` tabs (20×56 each, `WorkbenchScreen.java` lines 51-68), with the current page persisted on the block entity (`WorkbenchBlockEntity#isFabricationPageActive`) so reopening the screen restores the last-viewed tab. `dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md` line 189 already earmarks a further Duty 7 (point-spend) page as a third tab on this same bar — a Module tab on a machine's own screen is the identical pattern, just on a different block.
   - **Not yet shared code — flag this before copying it N times.** The `Page` enum and tab-blitting logic currently live entirely inside `WorkbenchScreen`/`WorkbenchMenu`; `AbstractModScreen`/`AbstractModMenu` have no tab infrastructure of their own. Copy-pasting the Workbench's `Page`/tab code into every opted-in machine's screen would duplicate the same layout math and click-handling repeatedly. Recommend generalizing a small tab-bar helper into `AbstractModScreen`/`AbstractModMenu` (an ordered list of tabs, each with an icon/label and a content-render callback) as part of step 4 below, so a machine opts into a Module tab by registering one entry, not by re-deriving the Workbench's bespoke `Page` mechanism. This mirrors the same "generalize out of the first implementation" move already planned for the Module *data* layer itself (step 2 below, `EaterItem` → shared code) — the UI needs the identical treatment, not just the backend.
   - **Slot content/filtering:** follows the same "scarce, valuable slot" convention already established for the Workbench's own Mod tab (`mayPlace` restricted to gadgets/Module Frames/exchangeable parts/`Damageable` items, `dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md` line 80) — a machine's Module tab should filter to `module/*`-tagged items only, consistent with Modules being identified by item tag rather than a bespoke per-consumer allowlist (see "3. Modules" above).
3. **Sequencing** (unchanged from the conversational pass, restated here for the written record):
   1. ~~Lock decision #1 above~~ — done.
   2. **✅ Done (2026-08-19) — Module infrastructure generalized out of `EaterItem`.** New `interfaces/IHaveModules.java`: a pure static-utility interface (no abstract methods, matching `IHaveItemData`'s own shape) holding `hasModule`, `installedHazardProfile`, and `buildModuleSlots` — each takes the consumer's `DataComponentType<BulkItemData>`/slot count explicitly rather than reading them off an instance, since Eater's own checks run from `private static` per-tick scan contexts with no `this` to dispatch through. Also hoisted `IHaveItemData#liveHandler` (the "re-resolve against a live stack supplier" wrapper) out of `EaterItem` into `IHaveItemData` itself, since it's generically useful beyond Modules and Eater's own buffer handler needed the identical wrapper. `EaterItem` now `implements IHaveModules` and its `installedHazardProfile`/`hasModule` are thin wrappers delegating to the shared statics — every existing call site in the class is unchanged. **`installedHazardProfile` now takes an explicit `base` `HazardProfile` parameter** (Eater passes `HazardProfile.TIER_1`, matching its current no-permanent-tier state) rather than hardcoding TIER_1 inside the shared method — this is a correctness fix for future consumers with a real permanent tier, not just a mechanical extraction: a Safety Module must union onto whatever the gadget already tolerates, never reset it to TIER_1. **Not done as part of this step, deliberately:** no rename of `MODULE_DATA` to an Eater-prefixed name (e.g. `EATER_MODULE_DATA`) even though that would match the mod's "separate registration per consumer" convention more closely — left alone to avoid unrelated churn; flagged so step 3 (Drinker) registers its own distinct component rather than reusing Eater's.
   3. **✅ Done (2026-08-19) — Drinker wired onto the shared Module infrastructure.** New `ModDataComponentTypes.DRINKER_MODULE_DATA` (Drinker's own distinct registration, never reusing Eater's `MODULE_DATA`, per that field's own flag from step 2). `DrinkerItem` gets **1 general-purpose Module slot** (per your call — deliberately smaller than Eater's 3, since Drinker's Module catalog is Safety-only, no mouthpiece-style specialties competing for the budget), plus a `DrinkerSwapPanel` (`implements IWorkbenchSwappable`) mirroring `EaterSwapPanel`'s pure-live-view shape minus the item-buffer slots (Drinker's buffer is a fluid tank with its own screen presentation, not part of this gadget-part panel). `DrinkerItem`'s previously-hardcoded `private static final HazardProfile PROFILE = HazardProfile.TIER_1` field is gone, replaced by a public `installedHazardProfile(ItemStack)` that unions `TIER_1` with whatever the installed Safety Module grants — **per your direction, this governs both targeting and containment**: the same profile gates `drainTank`/`accumulateSource` (the actual siphon) and is now also read by `DrinkerTargetScanner`'s action-bar readout (which previously kept its own separate hardcoded `PROFILE` copy — deleted, now calls `DrinkerItem.installedHazardProfile(drinker)` so the readout can never promise something the siphon then refuses). **Found and fixed while wiring this up:** `DrinkerItem.onItemUseFirst` intercepts a right-click on a valid siphon target *before* `use()` ever runs — without an explicit Scrench-pairing check there too, aiming Drinker at a machine/tank face while holding a Scrench would make the swap panel unreachable in exactly the moment a player most wants it (about to touch a hazardous fluid, wanting to check/swap a Safety Module first). Added the same other-hand Scrench check to `onItemUseFirst`, returning PASS to fall through to `use()`'s own check. `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`'s Drinker entry updated to match. **Compiled clean** (`./gradlew build`, verified locally 2026-08-19) — the "not compiled" note from the original cloud session no longer applies; no fixes were needed.
   3a. **✅ Done (2026-08-19) — S.I.P.P.I.N.G. wired onto the shared Module infrastructure, third consumer.** Same shape as Drinker's step 3: new `ModDataComponentTypes.SIPPING_MODULE_DATA`, 1 general-purpose Module slot, `SippingSwapPanel implements IWorkbenchSwappable`. `installedHazardProfile(stack)` now governs **both** fluid handlers `ModBusEvents` registers for Sipping (Storage's buffer AND Disposal's void) — previously both hardcoded to `HazardProfile.TIER_1` with no way to grant either one hazard tolerance, the exact gap Drinker had before its own step 3. Confirmed with you (2026-08-19): a Safety Module making Disposal mode able to void a hazardous fluid it couldn't before is the intended effect, not a gap to guard against — see `dermicraft-progression-notes.md`'s own player-responsibility stance echoed in memory. `SippingItem.use()` never had a Scrench-pairing check at all (unlike Drinker/Sunder/Shatter) — added it, since without it the swap panel was unreachable. Panel later got a fluid gauge + bidirectional fill/drain slot (same tank-above-slot art as Drinker's, but drains-then-falls-back-to-fills rather than drain-only, mirroring the existing `tryHandTransfer` hand-to-hand path's own priority order and `FluidUtil.tryFluidTransfer` mechanics) — Drinker's own panel doesn't need the fill direction since Transfer mode already covers "buffer into inventory containers," but Sipping has no comparable siphon-mode equivalent.
   3b. **Backlog, not yet started:** mode-cycle buttons in the GUI for both Drinker and Sipping (currently mode only cycles via crouch-right-click in the field, no GUI control), plus a shared tooltip convention for both items — current mode always shown, shift held reveals contained fluid + amount. Noted here so it isn't lost; explicitly deferred to keep the overhaul on its original sequencing.
   4. Build machine Module slots last, opt-in per family per Decision Point #2: a shared tab-bar helper in `AbstractModScreen`/`AbstractModMenu` (generalized off `WorkbenchScreen`'s `Page` pattern) plus the Module-tag-filtered slot content itself, since both halves are genuinely new (no block-entity or shared-screen precedent yet) rather than extensions of something already proven.
4. **✅ Resolved (2026-08-19) — leave both names as-is, document the split instead of renaming.** No rename this pass; revisit later if the collision keeps causing real confusion. The split, for the record (also belongs alongside the existing flag in `dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md` line 131):
   - **`HazardProfile`** — a **containment gate**. A set of tolerated hazard tags on a machine/tank/duct/floor/gadget; binary accept-or-refuse per container (`profile.accepts(fluidStack)`). Answers "can this thing hold this fluid at all." This is what Tier ladders and Modules (Safety Modules, Decision Points above) both operate on.
   - **Hazard Resistance / Hazard Defense** — two **player-facing suit stats** (`dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`). Don't gate anything — they soften a hazard's effect once the player is already exposed (Resistance: non-damaging effect timing/severity; Defense: damaging effect mitigation + Assault's H.E.A.T. discount). Answers "how much does this hurt me," never "am I allowed to touch this."
   - Both consume the same underlying hazard tags as shared vocabulary, which is exactly why the shared word "hazard" invites conflating them — keep this distinction in mind (and point back to this note) whenever either name comes up elsewhere in the overhaul, rather than re-deriving the split each time.

## Open questions (not yet even at "decision point" stage)

- Whether Points, as a mechanism, ever needs its own flexibility story (e.g. respec), or whether Modules is deliberately the *only* flexible/reversible axis and Points staying permanent is the intended contrast.
- Whether a taxonomy-driven audit like this should also cover Sunder/Shatter's swappable-part pattern as a fourth named shape (worth naming if a third consumer besides Sunder/Shatter ever wants it) or stays a two-consumer special case indefinitely.
- Whether machine Points (a magnitude-upgrade currency for machines, mirroring gadgets/suits) is in scope for this overhaul at all, or whether machines are meant to stay Tier-ladder-only by design (unlike every other consumer class) — not discussed anywhere yet, including in `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`.
