# Dermicraft Progression System — Overhaul Notes

Running log for the progression-system overhaul: stretching the Module system's flexibility (see `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md` → "Modules — direction note") past hazard tolerance and past Eater, to a consistent mod-wide framework for how the player unlocks/customizes capability. Companion to `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`, `dermicraft-suit-notes.md`, `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`, and `dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`.

**Why this doc exists, separate from the others:** every other progression-adjacent doc describes its own consumer's numbers. This one is the cross-cutting pass — the taxonomy that decides *which mechanism* a given capability should use, audited against everything that already exists, so future capabilities (and reworks of existing ones) have a rule to check against instead of an ad hoc call each time.

---

## The three mechanism shapes (proposed framework, not yet ratified)

Everything in the mod that gates or grows player/gadget/suit/machine capability already reduces to one of three shapes. None of these are new — each already exists somewhere in the codebase or design corpus — the overhaul is naming them explicitly and auditing every consumer against the set, per the "build the taxonomy first" starting point already logged in the Modules direction note.

### 1. Tier ladder
Permanent, sequential, forced or semi-forced progression. Advancing consumes the previous tier's item/recipe cost; there's no "undo." Grants **capacity and access**, not fine-grained choice.

- Code: `HazardProfile.TIER_1..TIER_4`, `MachineTier`, `NodeTier`, `TieredFloor`, gadget tiers (Eater/Drinker/Sprayer), suit Mark/Type/Series tiers.
- Mechanism: forced-evolution (Evolution Catalyst injection, dumb machines) or willing/button-press evolution (smart structures) or Growth Chamber (suits/gadgets, station not yet built — `dermicraft-project-primer.md` lines 159-174).
- What it answers: "What is this consumer *permanently* capable of right now."

### 2. Points
Permanent currency, spent once, never reclaimed. Buys either a smooth incremental stat rank or a discrete ability unlock. Each consumer (a suit, a gadget) holds its **own separate pool** — shared mechanic, not a shared currency (`dermicraft-gadget-notes.md` line 37).

- Code: none yet — design-only for Eater's material categories, M.A.W.S.'s features; suits' own point-spend menu at the Dock is further along but numbers aren't locked either.
- Earned via: fixed grant per tier-up, plus an escalating-cost buy-in once at max tier (same rule for suits and gadgets, not invented twice).
- What it answers: "How much / which permanent abilities has the player chosen to buy."

### 3. Modules
Destructible, physical, slot-limited items. Field-swappable (Scrench for gadgets; the suit's own swap path). Can **locally exceed** what tier/points alone would currently allow, and — where that's their purpose — are **self-resolving**: the same physical item gets consumed as a tier-up ingredient once the permanent tier catches up to what it was covering.

- Code: `MODULE_DATA`/`SAFETY_MODULE_PROPERTIES` on `EaterItem` only. Kind identified by item tag (`module/safety_heat`, `module/ore`, etc.), not a Java enum, so third-party items can join a category by datapack tag alone.
- **Resolved (2026-08-19):** the suit Add-on → "Module" rename is naming-only. Suit Modules (formerly Add-ons) keep their exact current behavior — permanent equipment, chosen from a roster, docked in the Add-on Frame, stays equipped until the player unequips it. They do **not** become destructible or self-resolving. Only the gadget-side Safety Modules/mouthpieces (and anything designed after them with the same destructible-and-self-resolving shape) carry that behavior.
- **Residual naming note:** "Module" is now a shared label over two genuinely different behavioral patterns — destructible/self-resolving (gadget Safety Modules, mouthpieces, Fluid Bypass) vs. permanent/equipped (suit Modules, ex-Add-ons). This is intentional (both are "an item in a scarce slot that defines a capability"), but it means the word alone doesn't tell you which behavior applies — always check the specific consumer's own doc rather than assuming destructibility (or permanence) from the name.
- **Third sub-shape confirmed (2026-08-19/20, design only, not built) — Evolution Modules, a family that drives gradual, self-resolving Tier evolution.** Renamed from "Production Module" (2026-08-20) once it became clear the fluid-switching behavior is only Drooling Cauldron's own local expression, not what the mechanism fundamentally is. **Crafted by upgrading an existing Hazard Module** (consumed as a recipe ingredient — a distinct item, never the same item wearing two hats) rather than reusing a Safety Module directly; this was an explicit design choice (see below) after reusing Heat Safety Module outright was considered and rejected. **Additive to Safety Modules, not a replacement** — a plain Safety Module still works exactly as built for a player who only wants today's hazard tolerance; an Evolution Module costs more and does more (the same immediate grant, plus a path to permanence). **Family, not one polymorphic item — gives each consumer what it needs, decided per consumer:** Drooling Cauldron's variant is a **selector** (switches current output immediately, a real behavioral difference from a union, not just a different consumer — see `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`'s Drooling Cauldron entry for the full mechanic) that separately accumulates processing-tick progress toward transforming into Drooling Crucible; Masticator's (and likely Effluentcer's/Metastasizer's, not yet individually designed) grants hazard tolerance the same way its Safety Module already does, additively, toward a permanent Tier 2 — **confirmed as the actual resolution to Masticator's already-flagged blocking reachability gap** (the entire Stage 2 Molten fluid family needs Lava/Thermal tolerance no Tier 1 Masticator has; see that entry in `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`). Both still fit "self-resolving" as already defined above (local capability ahead of where the permanent tier has caught up, consumed once it does) — what's new is that resolution is *gradual and tick-driven* rather than a one-shot ingredient consumption.
- **Rejected alternative (2026-08-20): reusing Heat Safety Module directly for the Cauldron's selector, rather than a new upgraded-from item.** Raw material cost wasn't the objection — it's that the same item would carry two incompatible consumption models (kept forever in a gadget's slot; consumed once a machine finishes evolving), which would also make "gadget hazard tolerance" and "machine Tier evolution" fully fungible resources despite being meant as separate progression axes. The upgraded-from-a-Hazard-Module shape avoids this: a distinct item, naturally priced above its base ingredient, with its own clear identity.
- What it answers: "What can this consumer do *right now, locally*, beyond its permanent tier/points state" — for the destructible sub-shape specifically, at the cost of the item's own survival; for the suit sub-shape, at the cost of a scarce slot only; for the selector/gradual-evolution sub-shape, at the cost of a scarce slot, all accumulated progress if pulled before it resolves, AND (corrected 2026-08-19, see the machine's own doc for the full mechanic) a dead period with no production at all until whatever the Module was making is fully drained out — pulling it is not a free instant undo back to the base output.

## Decision Points

1. **✅ Resolved (2026-08-19) — Suit Add-ons → Modules is rename-only.** "Add-on" becomes "Module" mod-wide in naming (suit terminology, the Add-on Frame → Module Frame, etc.), with zero behavioral change: suit Modules stay permanent equipment, chosen and kept, never destructible or self-resolving. Only the gadget-side Safety Modules/mouthpieces (and future designs sharing their purpose) carry the destructible/self-resolving behavior. **Doc-text rename pass done 2026-08-29, swept mod-wide.** `dermicraft-suit-notes.md`, `dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md`, and every doc cross-referencing the suit system (`dermicraft-project-primer.md`, `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`, `dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`, `dermicraft-liquid-foods-notes.md`, `dermicraft-slurry-notes.md`, `one-punch-full-breakdown.md`, `scout-blade-full-breakdown.md`) now use "Module"/"Module Frame" throughout, no behavioral change. Historical decision-log text describing the rename itself (this doc's own entries, and the direction-note in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md` that first proposed it) deliberately still says "Add-on," since it's quoting the old name as part of recording the decision, not describing current state. Code identifiers, item names, and lang keys are a separate, still-outstanding follow-up (not touched by the doc pass).
2. **✅ Resolved (2026-08-19) — machine Module slots live in their own screen tab, opt-in per family.** Confirms the opt-in direction already suggested here: a machine only gets Module slots if its screen has a Module tab, and building that tab is a per-machine-family choice — the farming family (hazard-free by design, `dermicraft-machine-notes.md` line 883) simply never gets one, no base-class flag or "off" switch needed.
   - **UI precedent to reuse, not invent:** the Workbench already has exactly this shape — `WorkbenchScreen`'s `Page` enum (`MOD`, `FABRICATION`) plus a vertically-stacked strip of `OPEN_TAB_TEXTURE`/`CLOSED_TAB_TEXTURE` tabs (20×56 each, `WorkbenchScreen.java` lines 51-68), with the current page persisted on the block entity (`WorkbenchBlockEntity#isFabricationPageActive`) so reopening the screen restores the last-viewed tab. `dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md` line 189 already earmarks a further Duty 7 (point-spend) page as a third tab on this same bar — a Module tab on a machine's own screen is the identical pattern, just on a different block.
   - **Not yet shared code — flag this before copying it N times.** The `Page` enum and tab-blitting logic currently live entirely inside `WorkbenchScreen`/`WorkbenchMenu`; `AbstractModScreen`/`AbstractModMenu` have no tab infrastructure of their own. Copy-pasting the Workbench's `Page`/tab code into every opted-in machine's screen would duplicate the same layout math and click-handling repeatedly. Recommend generalizing a small tab-bar helper into `AbstractModScreen`/`AbstractModMenu` (an ordered list of tabs, each with an icon/label and a content-render callback) as part of step 4 below, so a machine opts into a Module tab by registering one entry, not by re-deriving the Workbench's bespoke `Page` mechanism. This mirrors the same "generalize out of the first implementation" move already planned for the Module *data* layer itself (step 2 below, `EaterItem` → shared code) — the UI needs the identical treatment, not just the backend.
   - **Slot content/filtering:** follows the same "scarce, valuable slot" convention already established for the Workbench's own Mod tab (`mayPlace` restricted to gadgets/Module Frames/exchangeable parts/`Damageable` items, `dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md` line 80) — a machine's Module tab should filter to `module/*`-tagged items only, consistent with Modules being identified by item tag rather than a bespoke per-consumer allowlist (see "3. Modules" above).

4. **✅ Resolved (2026-08-19) — leave both names as-is, document the split instead of renaming.** No rename this pass; revisit later if the collision keeps causing real confusion. The split, for the record (also belongs alongside the existing flag in `dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md` line 131):
   - **`HazardProfile`** — a **containment gate**. A set of tolerated hazard tags on a machine/tank/duct/floor/gadget; binary accept-or-refuse per container (`profile.accepts(fluidStack)`). Answers "can this thing hold this fluid at all." This is what Tier ladders and Modules (Safety Modules, Decision Points above) both operate on.
   - **Hazard Resistance / Hazard Defense** — two **player-facing suit stats** (`dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`). Don't gate anything — they soften a hazard's effect once the player is already exposed (Resistance: non-damaging effect timing/severity; Defense: damaging effect mitigation + Assault's H.E.A.T. discount). Answers "how much does this hurt me," never "am I allowed to touch this."
   - Both consume the same underlying hazard tags as shared vocabulary, which is exactly why the shared word "hazard" invites conflating them — keep this distinction in mind (and point back to this note) whenever either name comes up elsewhere in the overhaul, rather than re-deriving the split each time.

## Open questions (not yet even at "decision point" stage)

- Whether Points, as a mechanism, ever needs its own flexibility story (e.g. respec), or whether Modules is deliberately the *only* flexible/reversible axis and Points staying permanent is the intended contrast.
- Whether a taxonomy-driven audit like this should also cover Sunder/Shatter's swappable-part pattern as a fourth named shape (worth naming if a third consumer besides Sunder/Shatter ever wants it) or stays a two-consumer special case indefinitely.
- Whether machine Points (a magnitude-upgrade currency for machines, mirroring gadgets/suits) is in scope for this overhaul at all, or whether machines are meant to stay Tier-ladder-only by design (unlike every other consumer class) — not discussed anywhere yet, including in `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`.
