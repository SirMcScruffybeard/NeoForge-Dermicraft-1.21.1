### Notebook — structure and scope (resolves prior open question)

**Scope (resolved):** Broad Stage 1 reference — not limited to the critical path to the Flesh Lab. Covers recipes, tools, mechanics, and hazard info across everything obtainable pre-Flesh Lab. Coverage philosophy: **broad but shallow by default** — every Stage 1 topic gets a clear functional explanation, but fine detail is reserved for cases where getting it wrong costs the player real time, resources, or safety (a "cost of ignorance" test, judged case-by-case as entries are written; Claude can flag candidates during drafting).

**Content rule (new, general):** Numeric precision for recipe **inputs** (exact quantities/ingredients). Qualitative-only for **outcomes** (yields, effects, power level) — keeps the Notebook useful for planning a craft without spoiling exact power-math.

**Unlock model (resolved):** All content available from the start, no discovery-gating — consistent with the Notebook's identity as fixed/finished content. (Hazard info's existing possession-gate and Stage 2 cap, already documented, are unchanged.)

**Structure (new):**
- A clickable **Table of Contents** at the front. Clicking an entry jumps to that section within one continuous book (not a separate page/view) — preserves the feel of a real notebook rather than a UI menu.
- **8 reference categories**, clean/functional only — no scientist voice, tips, or flavor of any kind live here. Order follows practical build-dependency (the natural read-through order for a player who doesn't jump ahead via the TOC), not narrative pacing:
  1. **Getting Started**
  2. **Tools**
  3. **Crafting Blends & Catalysts**
  4. **Slurries & Fuel**
  5. **Machines**
  6. **Gadgets**
  7. **Hazards**
  8. **The Flesh Lab**
- **Journal** — a separate, clearly-optional entry at the bottom of the TOC. This is the *sole* home for the scientist's voice, tips, and personality. Flavor-primary, with some practical tips mixed in. Carries the escalating tone arc and the goal-realization arc (see below). Reinforces the mod's existing "flavor never garbles function" rule structurally, not just typographically — a player who wants pure reference never has to encounter the scientist's voice at all.

### The scientist — characterization (new, resolves prior open questions)

**Core relationship to his work:** Obsessive/reckless — he pushes past where he should stop.

**Goal:** Immortality/godhood through strengthening and refining "the flesh" — but this is **not** a starting premise. It **emerges over the course of the Journal**: he begins doing the work for its own sake (interest, utility, "the job"), and only gradually realizes where it could lead. The goal is never flatly stated even once realized — it stays present under the surface, inferred rather than declared.

**Selfishness (deliberately ambiguous):** Whether his pursuit is self-interested or rationalized as benefiting others (science, humanity) is left unresolved — a man realizing this in real time likely wouldn't have a clean answer for his own motives, and giving him one would flatten the character.

**Tone arc:** Escalating — grounded and composed early in the Journal, gradually stranger/more unstable as it continues. This tracks his own proximity to whatever he pushed too far into, mirroring the player's own progression toward and past the Flesh Lab.

**Personality "screen time":** Moderate — a consistent background presence in the Journal, not a dominant narrative voice. Never present at all in the 8 clean categories.

**Possible eldritch influence:** Undecided, deliberately left open as a reserve explanation for his fate. If present, it is **invisible to and has zero mechanical influence on the player** — never hinted at through gameplay, never a mechanic. Cosmic horror is a mod element, not the focus.

**Continuity with existing lore:** This all sits behind his already-confirmed dark fate and final-boss return, and the existing Metaphysical (Mild) hallucination-table entry where he behaves differently from random doppelgangers — still the confirmed foreshadowing mechanism, unaffected by this session's additions.

### Getting Started — category content (drafted this session)

Three entries, in this order. ("How to Use This Book" as a separate entry was folded into "What This Is" and dropped — TOC/navigation is covered there.)

**1. What This Is**

> These notes are where I keep track of my discoveries and processes. Everything important is categorized in a way that is clear and efficient. The table of contents is at the front.
>
> The back of this notebook holds my thoughts. Not a necessary record, but the potential cost of losing anything is too great to exclude.

**2. Why the Flesh Lab**

> The Flesh Lab will be a facility used to create complex equipment and anything else I need. It will be directly compatible with the Gear Worx Stations, or function independently. I will be able to evolve it as I see fit.

**3. The Road Ahead**

> Tools come first: hand made, simple, clean, no special process required. They're what I use to build everything else.
>
> Next then building blocks: simple fuel, catalyst, and the meat of my machines, they will be hand made at the start. In a hole in the ground if need be.
>
> Then the machines: made from the bit and pieces. Some simple surgeries to bring them to life. Their industrial capabilities are a great stride forward.
>
> There are many damaging materials involved in this work. I've included what I've found in its own section.
>
> Finally: equipment to start expanding my own capabilities and the facilities to create and maintain them.

**Voice note on the 8 categories:** Even though they're clean of tips/personality, category entries are still **written in the scientist's first-person voice** (per the drafted entries above) — "clean" means no digressions, unreliability, or escalation, not third-person/impersonal instructional text. Getting Started, being first, establishes the baseline "grounded" register before the Journal's escalation ever begins.
