# Dermicraft — Project Primer

Hand this to Claude at the start of a new chat alongside any relevant per-topic log (e.g. `dermicraft-catalyst-notes.md`) to get back up to speed without re-explaining the project.

---

## What it is

**Dermicraft** is a Minecraft mod on **NeoForge, version 1.21.1**. It's a tech/factory mod with a **bio-horror** theme leaning toward a **mad science** feel — most blocks and items are a mix of living and non-living matter, and the framing favors experimentation, forced/unnatural processes, and lab-style logic over visceral gore. Horror is currently meant to come through mostly as **aesthetic flavor** rather than punishing gameplay mechanics (no confirmed sanity/infection/corruption systems as of this writing).

The mod is currently mid-**rebuild**: names, appearances, and mechanics are being reworked from an older version.

## Damage-reduction stacking (mod-wide rule)

Confirmed as a general rule across **all damage types**, not just fall damage: **every source of reduction for a given damage type stacks additively — unless one source completely negates that instance of damage, in which case that one source alone handles it** (nothing further needed once damage is fully negated). Applies to any current or future mechanic that reduces damage — suit passives, point-spend categories, add-ons (Landing Brace, Hero Landing, etc.), Toughness, and so on.

## Stage structure

- **Stage 1 (early game):** current focus. Stage 1 blocks/items cannot handle lava.
- **Stage 2:** introduces lava as a generated fluid/ingredient — **Lava itself is the formal first Stage 2 fluid.** The **Nether is the gate** into Stage 2 — Nether access itself is available in Stage 1 (for gathering Nether-sourced ingredients), it's specifically Dermicraft's own machines/fluids that can't handle lava until Stage 2. Stage 2 introduces the **Molten family** — Lava-based Crafting Blends that give rare/hard-to-liquefy materials (Redstone, Quartz, Glowstone, Amethyst, Diamond, Obsidian, Lapis, Netherite Scrap, Soul Sand) a fluid form, plus creature-drop "Essences" (Blaze, Ghast, Wither, Ender) and harvested Dragon's Milk. Full roster in `dermicraft-crafting-notes.md`.

**Tier system (Gadgets/Machines):** Separate from Stages. Gadgets and Machines each carry their own Tier rating; **Tier 1** cannot handle hazardous fluids (lava is the example given) unless a specific item notes otherwise. **Tier 2 is confirmed to exist**, and its main/defining upgrade over Tier 1 is specifically **lava capability** — e.g. a Tier 2 Sprayer can use lava as flamethrower fuel.

**Tier vs. Stage relationship — clarified:** **Tier operates at the micro level** — a capability rating on an individual item, Gadget, or Machine (e.g. lava-handling capability). **Stage describes the macro level** — the player's overall progress through the mod at major milestones (e.g. Nether access gating Stage 2 as a whole). The two are related but track separately: a Tier 2 device existing in the world doesn't by itself confirm where the *player* sits on overall Stage progression, and vice versa. Exact overlap between specific Tiers and specific Stages (e.g. is Tier 3/4 content gated behind a specific later Stage?) still isn't pinned down — see Living Catalyst in `dermicraft-catalyst-notes.md` for an open example currently sitting in this gap.

**Hazard tag hierarchy (confirmed):** `Hazardous` is a parent tag — Tier 1 blanket-excludes anything under it, which automatically covers any future hazard sub-type without needing per-tag updates to Tier 1's rule. Child tags so far: **Extreme Heat** (lava), **Radiation** (carrying its own internal **Mild** / **Severe** split), **Biohazard**, and **Metaphysical** (also split **Mild** / **Severe**, added speculatively — no Stage timing assigned yet, but now has its first confirmed use: **Molten Soul Silica** carries Metaphysical Mild, see `dermicraft-crafting-notes.md`). **Tier 2's defining capability is specifically Extreme Heat handling, not the parent `Hazardous` tag as a whole** — a machine engineered to survive molten heat doesn't automatically block radiation, the same way a real firefighter's suit and a lead apron solve two unrelated problems. Radiation-handling is tracked as its own, separately-gated capability: **Mild Radiation** (Living Glowstone) is intended to be handled by Living Metals once they exist (see Living Catalyst in `dermicraft-catalyst-notes.md`); **Severe Radiation** (Ascendant Catalyst) has no confirmed handling solution yet. **Biohazard** is the newest confirmed-in-use child tag, arriving alongside Severe Radiation. **Metaphysical is deliberately off the Extreme Heat → Radiation → Biohazard ladder** — it isn't slotted into any Tier's cumulative rule and isn't implied by any confirmed Tier/Stage yet.

**Implementation note (as-built, code-level):** hazard tolerance is implemented as a `HazardProfile` — a **set** of tolerated hazard-kind tags per tank/duct/machine, not a linear tier integer. The Tier 1–4 cumulative ladder above is still expressed faithfully as named presets (`TIER_1`…`TIER_4`, each adding exactly the hazards this section lists), but the set model additionally allows **off-ladder combinations** — a container can tolerate a non-ladder hazard (e.g. either Metaphysical split) without that implying anything about its position on the Heat/Radiation/Biohazard ladder. This is *why* Metaphysical could be added without touching the Tier 1–4 definitions at all. Weakest-link composition (Floor paths, duct runs — see below and `dermicraft-innards-duct-notes.md`) is a set **intersection** rather than a `min()` of tier numbers, which is the set-model equivalent of the same rule.

**Hazard-to-Stage rough timing:** Extreme Heat gates Stage 1→2; **Mild Radiation** appears at the **late Stage 2 / early Stage 3** boundary; **Severe Radiation and Biohazard** arrive in the **late Stage 3 / Stage 4** range (the 2→3 boundary being deliberately fuzzier than the clean 1→2 lava gate). This same hazard ladder defines the FL's floor-tier fluid-conduction gating — a floor must be tier-appropriate to conduct each hazard class (Tier 1 safe → Tier 2 lava → Tier 3 mild radiation → Tier 4 severe radiation + biohazard). See `dermicraft-machine-notes.md` → Floor Block (Floor Tiers).

**Tools aren't Tier-gated — confirmed precedent.** Tier ratings only ever applied to Gadgets/Machines, never to Tools — this is exactly why Flask of Lava already worked without contradicting Tier 1's hazard restriction (a Tool can safely contain/transport a hazardous fluid in small amounts; only active machine-handling of it is Tier-gated). The same logic now extends to allowing small, difficult-to-produce amounts of Living Catalyst/Living Glowstone to exist before any Tier 3/4 Machine exists to handle them at scale.

**Tier 1 as "tutorial" framing.** The Tier 1 → Tier 2 transition, gated specifically by Extreme Heat (lava) capability, is treated as a deliberate narrative signal — crossing it marks the player leaving an early "tutorial" phase and entering the mod's "real game." Reinforced by Primitive Catalyst and its entire early crafting chain (Carbon/Calcium/Protein/Metal Blends, Evolution Catalyst's original Stage-1 recipe) being confirmed to live entirely within Tier 1 — the complete early-game toolkit exists before that gate, not scattered across it.

**Tier 2 visual/material theme (flagged, not finalized).** Early Stage 2 Tier 2 machines are envisioned with an **Obsidian carapace/shell** look — Obsidian being literally cooled, hardened lava, so the machine visibly carries proof it survived the heat it's rated to handle (fits Machines' "living to some degree" framing as a grown response to heat exposure, rather than just a material choice). Later Stage 2 machines are envisioned incorporating more metal as Living Metals come online, specifically to handle Mild Radiation. Exact machines and Tier numbers not yet assigned.

**Stage 3 (first defined).** Centers on scaling Living Catalyst / Living Metal production from a difficult, low-yield trace-access state — introduced at the Stage 2/3 boundary, alongside Living Glowstone — up to real, repeatable production. Mirrors the same "late recipe upgrades an early one" pattern used elsewhere (e.g. Evolution Catalyst's two recipes), just applied at the Stage level instead of the recipe level — Stage boundaries aren't instant flips; there's a transition zone where late-Stage-2 and early-Stage-3 content overlap.

**Future stages flagged, not defined:** Nether-sourced and End-sourced "rock" materials are both confirmed excluded from Tier 1 Sediment Blend duplication (see Fluids → Crafting below, and `dermicraft-crafting-notes.md`), deferred to later Stages — implying at least one Stage beyond Stage 2 exists for Nether materials, and a separate later milestone (or the same one) for the End. Neither is defined beyond this exclusion.

## Construction philosophy (resolved — deliberate)

The three-way **Tools / Machines / Gadgets** construction split is deliberate, not accidental. It's a **dependency ladder**, not three parallel lanes — how a thing is built maps to what it is and roughly where it sits in progression:

- **Tools** → vanilla **crafting table**. Mundane, **non-living**, no special infrastructure — the earliest things, and the very instruments used to build everything else.
- **Machines** → the **tumor-genesis chain**, completed by **Syringe injection** (and the Syringe/Suture Kit are themselves Tools). So Machines are built *using* Tools.
- **Gadgets** → **FL-crafted**; the FL is itself the apex Machine, so Gadgets are built *by* a Machine. (The **Tablet is a Gadget** — no separate category for it.)

Each rung is enabled by the one before it (Tools → build Machines → the apex Machine is the FL → builds Gadgets), which yields three clean rules: (1) build-method identifies category and rough tier; (2) no cross-contamination — a Tool shouldn't need the FL, a Gadget shouldn't be crafting-table-made; (3) Tools being "just crafting-table-made" is the *intended baseline*, not a missing mechanic.

**The real law is the living-matter through-line.** The construction ladder is the usual *signal*, but the deeper rule is: **everything more complex than a simple Tool uses living matter in some way** — simple Tools are the non-living baseline, everything above is alive to some degree. The *kind* of living matter shifts with the Stages: **organic flesh** early (tumors, nerve clusters, dense muscle), transitioning toward **living metals and living materials** later (Living Metals, Living Glowstone — ties into the hazard/Stage ladder above). So "is it living?" is the true line between simple Tools and everything else, and "what kind of living matter?" tracks Stage progression.

**Named exceptions/bridges:**
- **The FL** — a Machine whose whole job is building Gadgets; the ladder's deliberate hinge, not a violation.
- **Nose on a Stick** — the clearest rule-breaker: it functions as a Tool/Gadget but is built via an **implant recipe** (the Machine construction method). It breaks the build-method↔category mapping — yet still *confirms* the living-matter law (implant = living construction), which is exactly why the living-matter through-line, not the build method, is treated as the real rule.

## Machines

**Status:** Core mechanic established; category defined relative to Gadgets; well over a dozen machines now named across operational builds, committed concepts, and early-planning ideas — full detail in the companion doc.

**Definition:** Block-form devices that handle tasks — the stationary counterpart to Gadgets (same "handles tasks" role, different form factor: block vs. hand-held). All Machines discussed so far will be Tier 1 (see Stage structure for what that restricts). Usually single blocks, though at least one (Flesh Lab) is a multiblock structure.

Machines are framed as **living to some degree** — and this is now confirmed as mechanically literal, not just flavor. **Every Machine originates from an Inert Tumor block**, built by placing that Machine's specific recipe items into the tumor (in its Marred state), stitching it closed, and injecting it — most likely with Primitive Catalyst — via Syringe. Full mechanic in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-tools-notes.md`**. Drooling Cauldron and Masticator already have tumor recipes defined (flagged for fine-tuning); Skin Tank doesn't yet (blocked on a missing ingredient); every other Machine below still needs one written.

Upgrading a machine is a **forced evolution** process, not a simple tier-up — a separate, later mechanic from the tumor-genesis step above:
- Each machine has its own evolution recipe — specific items/fluids stored in the machine over time.
- Final step: inject **Evolution Catalyst** to complete the transformation.
- A successful evolution swaps the machine for a **unique new block** (visual/model details deferred to a later discussion — not yet designed).

**Machine Evolution — Smart vs. Dumb (mod-wide rule, confirmed):** ordinary "dumb" Machines (Masticator, Effluentcer, Metastasizer, Skin Tank/Chitin Tank, and small machines generally) only ever evolve via **forced** Evolution Catalyst injection — the standard, default path, and permanent for their whole family lineage. "Smart" structures qualify by a checkable rule instead of a judgment call: **a Brain (or Brain-derived ingredient) is required somewhere in the structure's recipe** — true only of the FL's control block and the Gear Worx Stations, both of which get **willing** evolution (button-press, fluid-paid, no forced injection, never damaged by an interrupted attempt). The same split governs fuel-empty behavior: dumb Machines keep grinding unfueled at 1/10th rate until their HP drains to 0; smart structures have no HP and simply halt cleanly, preserving progress. Most Tier 1→2 "dumb" evolutions follow one standard template (Lava Bucket + a tank full of Water + Evolution Catalyst injection, everything consumed) — a deliberate echo of vanilla obsidian generation, and consistent with the Tier 2 "obsidian carapace" visual theme above.

**Known machines:**
- **Skin Tank** — simple bulk fluid storage, 10-bucket (10,000 mB) capacity. **Chitin Tank** is its confirmed Tier 2 evolved form (20,000 mB, doubling again at each subsequent tier) — a general-purpose Tier 2 tank, not tied to any single recipe.
- **Drooling Cauldron** — passively generates water (1 mB/sec); also converts food items into bonus water, scaled to the food's nutrition/saturation.
- **Masticator** — blends one fluid + one item into a different output fluid; confirmed as the producer of all current Crafting fluids — the original six Blends plus the newer Sediment Blends (Stone/Silica/Clay) and the Blood Nugget → Ferrous Blend alternate route (see Fluids → Crafting below).
- **Effluentcer** — **operational.** Mixes two fluids into one; three recipes live: F-Stuff (Crude Slurry + Protein Blend, also cookable/smokable into a food item called MRE, and Metastasizer-duplicable once bootstrapped), C-Stuff (Carbon Blend + Calcium Blend, now also with its own standalone Syringe-based secondary use on Creepers — see Tools below), and F-Stuff + C-Stuff → Primitive Catalyst.
- **Metastasizer** — **operational.** Block, block entity, menu, and screen are all implemented, and a wide roster of duplication recipes is live: all 17 Sediment Blend roster items (Stone/Silica/Clay families), the Metal Blends' reverse route (Ferrous/Cuprous/Aurous Blend → Ingot/Nugget, mirroring the Masticator's forward recipes 1:1), MRE (F-Stuff) and the new Meat Flavored Meat (Protein Blend), and Protein Blend → Inert Tumor/Dense Muscle/Nerve Cluster/Eye duplication. Takes a fluid + an item; the item is normally a non-consumed pattern that gets duplicated, confirmed as the *default* behavior rather than a hard rule — recipes can define a different output entirely (e.g. the still-unimplemented Protein Blend + Iron Nugget → Blood Nugget concept). Works on a recipe-by-recipe basis, not generically.
- **Gestator** — concept stage only. A new machine distinct from the Metastasizer (the name was previously attached to the Metastasizer before being reassigned here). Takes Living Catalyst and one other fluid. If the second fluid is non-living and a living counterpart exists, it produces the living version of that fluid. If the second fluid is already living, it produces a large batch of it — visually resembling a greatly accelerated replication rate. Invalid fluid input (no living counterpart exists yet) is not refused outright — the fluid pools inside the machine and must be manually extracted, though a dedicated Gadget makes this easy; automation can also pump it out. Runs without Slurry fuel but slowly; Slurry speeds the process up. Operates on an internal tube-driven timing cycle rather than continuous fuel draw. Whether this fuel-optional behavior becomes a mod-wide Machine pattern is a live open question for a dedicated future session.
- **Drooling Crucible** — concept stage only. First confirmed **Tier 2** machine; the forced-evolution result of the Drooling Cauldron, producing lava instead of water.
- **Mr. Farmer, Mr. Shepard, Mr. Logger, Mr. Hunter** — a family of related early-planning ideas (crop, animal, tree, and mob-grinding automation, respectively), preserved for future consideration but not all committed concepts. Mr. Farmer is the furthest along — conceived as a machine resembling a human head in pain placed in/above a garden plot, and now the first with a real implementation (see `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`). Mr. Hunter is bare concept-only so far. Where any of the four fit in the Stage/Tier timeline is undecided.
- **Render Furnace & Grafting Table** — **operational, both implemented and playtested.** Deliberately minor, Tier 1-only early-game convenience machines — the mod's first to run on **vanilla's own recipe books** (`RecipeType.SMELTING`/`RecipeType.CRAFTING`) instead of a Dermicraft recipe type, with zero datagen of their own. Positioned as training wheels, not a parallel economy: a real machine (Masticator/Metastasizer/Mutator) for the same output is always meant to end up cheaper and duct-automatable once available. Both use a hard fuel gate (`MachineTier.NO_HEALTH`, no HP bar) instead of the usual fuel-optional/HP-drain template — unfueled, they freeze rather than limp along and take damage. Implant recipes skip flesh entirely (just the vanilla Furnace/Crafting Table, sutured, + Primitive Catalyst) — the cheapest implants in the mod. Full detail, plus a separate implementation build-order doc, in `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`.
- **Craw** — **operational.** Single-item-type bulk storage (10 stacks / 640 items), item-side counterpart to Skin Tank. Block, block entity, menu, and screen are all implemented, along with its implant recipe. Two interaction paths: drawer-style right-click on the block (regular = one item, crouch = whole stack, both ways) and a GUI (via Outerface) with an input slot that feeds the storage slot. Used as FL output storage (flanking the Core) and as a crafting-step vessel in the Core's own construction recipe. Full detail in `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`.
- **Flesh Lab** ("FL") — a multiblock structure (core block + floor blocks; connected machines/storage in range feed it). **Confirmed as the standard construction path for Gadgets** (though no longer the *sole* one — the Gear Worx Stations' Workbench can also fabricate Gadgets using the same recipes, see `dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md`), and more broadly as a universal crafting front-end: if the FL itself or any attached Machine has a recipe for something, the FL can craft it — not limited to Gadgets. The first example (the Tablet, built from the Notebook + other ingredients) is confirmed. **Built across Stage 1 and Stage 2, not a pure Tier 1 capstone** — the Core (its control block) requires an Evolution Catalyst injection step that isn't reachable until Stage 2, making the FL a bridge project the player starts early and finishes after crossing the Nether gate. Still the de facto goal/milestone of Tier 1 in the sense that its foundation (Floor, initial storage) is Stage-1-buildable. Full detail, including the Core's two-stage construction recipe and the Floor/Craw component blocks, in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-machine-notes.md`**.
- **Imago Engine** (working name was "Network Evolver") — concept stage only. Solves mod-wide tier-upgrade tedium: a small controller + floor + storage multiblock that batch-evolves every connected Machine (and Innards Ducts/Nodes) below a selected target tier, paying costs in fluids from its own storage, closest-first, over time rather than instantly. **Structural mastery gate:** the Engine must itself already be evolved to Tier N to *hold* Tier N's payment fluids, so a player's first evolution at each tier is always manual — batch automation only unlocks after that. Every machine keeps its manual and FL-craftable routes alongside this as a third, convenience-layer option.
- **Mutator** — concept fully brainstormed, not yet implemented. Takes fuel + a fluid + an item; a GUI mode toggle switches between **Mutate mode** (fuel + reagent fluid + item → a different, more complex item, reagent consumed — e.g. Fluid Bladder + Cuprous Blend → Fuel Bladder) and **Fill mode** (fluid + any fluid-handling container → the same item now holding that fluid as cargo, no fuel cost — this is the absorbed **Filling Station** concept, no longer a standalone machine). Recipes are derived from existing crafting-table recipes by mapping ingredients to their nearest fluid analog, Metastasizer-style. Several recipes (the Mossy/Stained-Glass/Terracotta "overgrowth"/ceramics families) are already implemented. Full detail, plus a separate implementation build-order doc, in `dermicraft-machine-notes.md`.

Full reasoning for all of the above lives in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-machine-notes.md`**.

**Open questions:** How many machines are planned before Stage 2 hits? Does every machine evolve, or only some (Drooling Cauldron → Drooling Crucible is the first confirmed real example)? Does each Machine pair with a Gadget equivalent, or are they independent lists?

## Innards Ducts

**Status:** Both block types (Innards Duct and Node) are implemented and feature-complete for Tier 1 — remaining work is playtesting, same as the rest of the mod, not further design/implementation. Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-innards-duct-notes.md`**.

**Definition:** A lightweight, cheap automation-transport system — the mod's answer to hoppers/pipes for longer-distance or branching item/fluid movement. Two block types: the **Innards Duct** (a dumb, single-purpose conduit segment, no routing logic of its own) and the **Node** (the only place routing decisions happen). No simulated travel through the pipe and no per-tick graph walks — a duct run resolves once to its two endpoints and transfers directly between them on a ticked cadence.

**Node is mandatory for any flow.** A duct run has no way to infer source/sink on its own — direction is only ever set explicitly via a Node's per-leg In/Out/Off button. A run with no Node anywhere on it is simply inert.

**Node routing:** each connected leg gets independent **In/Out/Off** state plus independent **Item and Fluid on/off toggles** (both default off) — a leg can carry both, either, or neither. A 1-item/1000 mB buffer acts as a two-phase pull/push relay. Global per-Node distribution mode is **Round-Robin or Equal Spread** across enabled outputs — no priority list.

**Size cap:** 64 blocks per run, enforced as a placement-time rejection — keeps a run feeling like a local conduit, not a base-spanning logistics backbone.

**Upgrades planned for both block types** (not yet designed) — both are built with inheritance-ready abstract base classes from the start for this reason.

**Open questions:** Upgrade-tier specifics for both blocks. See the companion doc for the full rendering/texture/lore rationale.

## Gadgets

**Status:** Category defined relative to Tools/Machines/Weapons. Gadgets now has a dedicated **Weapons subsection**. Several new Gadgets added this session: Eater, Lobber, Chainsword, Drill Hammer, Grappling Hook, Capture Net Gun, plus the full Suit line (Exo-skeleton, Assault, Recon — see their own section below).

**Definition:** Hand-held devices that handle tasks — more complex than Tools, and the hand-held counterpart to Machines (same "handles tasks" role, different form factor).

**Known gadgets:**
- **S.I.P.P.I.N.Gadget** ("Sipper") — carried over from the old version; needs reworking. Function defined: shaped like a tin can, placed into a Machine to transfer or dispose of the fluid inside it. 1000 mB capacity. Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`**.
- **D.R.I.N.K.E.Rig** ("Drinker") — carried over from the old version; needs reworking. Function defined: a fluid vacuum with a 1000 mB tank and three modes (Disposal/Storage/Transfer). Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`**.
- **S.P.R.A.Y.E.Rig** ("Sprayer") — concept stage only, not yet built. A spray gun whose effect depends on the fluid in its tank (Water = anti-Enderman "flamethrower" + crop watering; Calcium Blend = area bone meal). A Tier 2 version is planned to use lava as an actual flamethrower fuel. Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`**.
- **Eater** — concept stage, core mechanics defined. The item-side counterpart to Drinker: vacuums loose dropped items at base tier, loose blocks (dirt/sand/gravel, speed-upgradeable) at mid tier, and ore directly out of ore blocks (leaving the base stone-equivalent behind) at a later tier, via a distance-widening directional cone. Confirmed as an acronym (expansion pending). Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`.
- **Lobber** — concept stage. A fluid grenade launcher; ammo is a new dedicated shatter-on-impact container (not Flask), and fluid effects mirror Sprayer's exactly. Aim high to lob in an arc, aim flat for a direct short-range shot. Single-shot with manual reload to start; magazine capacity planned as a later upgrade. Confirmed as an acronym (expansion pending). Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`.
- **Grappling Hook** — concept stage. Traversal Gadget modeled on Deep Rock Galactic's grapple: fires at a valid anchor point (block or mob) and pulls the player directly to it at speed, momentum carrying through on arrival. Slurry-fueled, cost charged at reload; better fuel grade improves range, projectile speed, pull speed, and reduces projectile drop. Can target mobs — resolved by **mass**, not combat power: lower-mass mobs get pulled to the player, higher-mass mobs (including all bosses) pull the player to them, letting it double as a way to close distance on flying bosses like the Ender Dragon. No PvP grappling for now. Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`.
- **Capture Net Gun** — concept stage. Non-lethal capture Gadget; fires a net that turns a mob into a spawn-egg-style item (auto-added to inventory) instead of killing it. Slurry-fueled, same reload-cost rule as the Grapple. Five tiers, each capturing everything at or below it: Passive mobs → Early hostiles → Basic Nether mobs → Powerful non-boss mobs → Bosses (Ender Dragon, Wither, Warden). Capture is guaranteed on hit with the correct tier, once the target (including bosses) is genuinely battle-ready. Released via right-click on the ground, spawn-egg style. Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`.
- **The Tablet** — concept stage. An intelligent, **living**, self-authoring Gadget — the second half of the mod's two-part in-game guide system (Notebook → Tablet). FL-crafted from the Notebook plus other ingredients (a confirmed first example of the FL's Gadget-crafting role); Notebook content carries over. Physically connected to the player (lore-confirmed, mechanism undecided). Supports manual lock/unlock of content sections for decluttering. No operating cost while purely informational. Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`; narrative/tone context in `dermicraft-misc-notes.md`.
- **Injection Gun** — concept fully specified, not yet implemented. A craft-up replacement for the plain Syringe: a 5-cell revolver-style injector (500 mB/cell, 100 mB/dose) so different fluids or repeat doses no longer mean carrying multiple Syringes. Deliberately framed as a **Tool/Gadget bridge** — purely mechanical (no Proto Brain), but Gadget-tier presentation (GeckoLib model, HP). Gated by its own positive-allowlist `injectable` fluid tag, independent of the general `HazardProfile` system. Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`.
- **MAWS** (Mobile Autonomous Ware Storage) — concept fully specified, not yet implemented. A handheld mass-item-storage gadget (fixed per-tier slot grant: 9/18/27..., device-wide stack-cap multiplier) usable without placing it down — closer to a carry-around Craw than to Sipper. Its own separate point-spend pool (suit-style mechanic, not shared currency). Includes an automatic **Bladder hot-swap** (dead Ammo/Feeder Bladders in inventory auto-swap for full ones from MAWS storage). Full detail in `dermicraft-gadget-notes.md`.

**Weapons subsection (new):**
- **Chainsword** — a Warhammer 40k-inspired melee weapon, built organic to fit Dermicraft's aesthetic. FL-crafted, recipe flagged as easy to iterate. One-hits 1-block-wide trees; high Bleed chance on hit; guaranteed mechanical decapitation on a killing blow (head drops alongside standard drops). Slurry-fueled: grade affects performance, fuel heals the weapon (no conventional durability loss while fueled). Unfueled fallback drains player hunger per swing (base) or per hit (Safety upgrade) — mutually exclusive with an alternate upgrade that instead makes it act like a mundane sword (reduced stats, takes durability damage, no hunger drain).
- **Drill Hammer** — a slow-swinging mining/combat weapon. 3×3 face AoE, center 2 blocks deep, edges 1 deep — same footprint for mining and combat. Hitting a mob restricts the AoE to mobs only; missing hits the first block in line and applies the AoE to blocks; flying out of reach does nothing. Heavy bonus damage vs. skeletons/wither skeletons (possible extra drops/status effect, TBD) and heavy bonus durability damage to armor except chainmail. Heads are swappable via the shared Gadget customization system, following a vanilla-inspired material/damage curve. Same fuel relationship as Chainsword.

Sipper and Drinker are Tier 1. Sprayer's base version is also Tier 1, with a Tier 2 upgrade planned (see Stage structure for what Tier governs).

**Naming note:** All Gadgets follow an acronym + suffix pattern, where the acronym's last letter is the first letter of the gadget's full written-out word. "Eater," "Lobber," "Chainsword," and "Drill Hammer" are discussion shorthand; Eater and Lobber are confirmed as real acronyms with expansions still pending. Simplified shorthand names are used in discussion going forward, but full acronym names stay canonical for documentation — full naming-convention note lives in the companion doc.

**Open questions:** Does each Gadget need/have a Machine counterpart? Eater and Lobber's full acronym expansions. Lobber's magazine-upgrade mechanism and possible Tier 2 (lava) version.

## Suits

**Status:** Concept stage, but deeply developed — three suits, a shared add-on/slot/tier/point system, per-suit inherent traits and signature weapons, and a suit-wide H.E.A.T. resource are all confirmed. Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-suit-notes.md`**; the two Assault/Recon signature weapons each have their own dedicated breakdown doc (see below).

**Definition:** Three wearable, fully-customizable suit lines, sharing the same FL-born origin and slot-based add-on customization philosophy as Gadgets. All three occupy the full four vanilla armor slots as one locked-in unit (no mixing pieces between suits or with vanilla armor).

**Known suits:**
- **Exo-skeleton** — the free base suit. Permanently has no defense (aside from a possible late-game add-on exception). Functions fully unfueled with no drawback, granting modest passive movement/jump/fall/swing-mining-speed/hunger-use boosts. Fuel (Concentrated grade and above) additionally boosts these passive stats; Crude Slurry powers add-ons only. 6 slots at Tier 0, evenly distributed (1 per body part). **Defining feature (confirmed):** as it tiers up, Exo gains *more* total add-on slots than Assault or Recon — its answer to trading away combat/mobility ceiling — plus two bridge equipment pieces, **H.E.A.T. Sink** and **Overdrive Core**, that let it slot the *other* suits' exclusive add-ons (never their inherent suit-signature traits, which stay permanently exclusive to their origin suit).
- **Assault** — heavy combat fork. Near-unarmored base movement, but gains Heavy Strike (bonus combat/mining damage for reduced swing speed), reduced knockback taken, and — starting at Tier 1 (which also fills in Assault's first Head slot, previously empty at Tier 0) — Toughness, its signature defensive trait. 6 slots at Tier 0, concentrated in Arms (2 each) with no Head slot. Requires fuel to function normally; drains player hunger when fuel runs out. **From Tier 1 onward, the suit survives player death and stays equipped through respawn** (banked H.E.A.T. is still wiped). Signature weapon: **One Punch** — banks a suit-wide resource called **H.E.A.T.** (gained from the unarmored-equivalent damage of hits taken) and discharges the full bank as a multiplier on the next empty-hand punch; full mechanic in `one-punch-full-breakdown.md`. Also gains the inherent **Reactive Defense** trait (a H.E.A.T.-costed damage-mitigation burst on being hit) and the wall/mob-tunneling **Juggernaut** add-on (a continuous, partial H.E.A.T. + fuel drain).
- **Recon** — light mobility fork. Retains real (if reduced) defense, toned-down Exo-like passive mobility stats, and the exclusive **Running Gear** add-on (renamed from "Increased Dash Speed"). Uniquely can run both Flight-family and both Climbing-family add-ons simultaneously, without the early exclusivity restriction the other suits carry. 6 slots at Tier 0, concentrated in Head (2) and Legs (2), with only 1 shared Arm slot. Same fuel-required/hunger-fallback behavior as Assault. Signature weapon: **Scout Blade** (renamed from "Recon Blade") — Recon's empty-hand attack once unlocked at Tier 1, dealing damage multiplied by the player's peak recent speed, uncapped; full mechanic in `scout-blade-full-breakdown.md`. Also gains the inherent **Slipstream** trait (a speed-scaled, uncapped chance to dodge an incoming hit entirely) and exclusive fall add-ons **Hero Landing**/**Hell Dive** (mutually exclusive with each other).

**Design intent (confirmed):** A three-way trade-off, not a power ladder — Exo trades ceiling for freedom, Recon trades defense for mobility, Assault trades mobility for raw power.

**Universal add-ons** span Head (perception), Chest (power/utility core — also representing the torso and, functionally, part of the arm-slot logic), Arms, and Legs, including Flight, Climbing (two distinct styles), Auto Shield, the merged **Auto Heal** add-on (folds the former Self-Healing + Player Regen + Safe Removal into one, now available to all three suits with no Exo exclusion), Water Striding, Lava Wading, Lava Striding, and more — full roster and slot-eligibility rules in the companion doc.

**Design reference:** The universal add-on/slot system is confirmed to be a simplified version of the decoration-socket philosophy from modern Monster Hunter — scarce, valuable slots, and specialization through what's equipped rather than a fixed build.

**Hazard Resistance / Hazard Defense (confirmed):** two suit-wide stats every suit carries (Exo's baseline is 0 for both, consistent with its zero-defense identity), governing how hazard exposure actually feels to the player rather than the hard machine-side accept/reject `HazardProfile` gate. **Hazard Resistance** covers non-damaging hazard effects (timing and severity); **Hazard Defense** covers damaging ones, and for Assault also discounts the raw hazard damage banked as H.E.A.T. Each suit expresses the same two stats differently (Assault: strength-based; Recon: timing-based; Exo: add-on-only). Full detail, including the general H.E.A.T.-generation rule for hazards (outside-in contact damage generates it, inside-out deterioration damage like Radiation/Biohazard doesn't), in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`**.

**Progression:** Layered as base stats → tier adjustments (loosely modeled on vanilla's armor-material curve) → point-spend upgrades, in that order. New add-on slots unlock on a fixed schedule (one every second tier) rather than being point-purchased. Unassisted (rushed) suit removal costs the player damage, scaling linearly with tier, starting at 1 point of damage at Tier 1; assisted removal via a dedicated safe-removal dock (see Gear Worx Stations below) costs time instead, at a ratio of 1 second per point of would-be damage. A fully-developed, unmitigated suit at max tier is calibrated so unassisted removal deals damage equal to or exceeding the player's effective max health — functionally equivalent to death through the normal damage system, not a scripted instant-kill, and avoidable entirely through planning (the dock, dedicated mitigation add-ons, or point investment).

**Open questions:** Exact per-tier removal-damage values beyond Tier 1. Final-tier point-purchase currency and cost curve. Whether Flight/Glide and Climbing exclusivity ever lifts for Exo/Assault. Whether Exo ever gains defense via a late-game add-on. Exact numbers for Reactive Defense/Slipstream, Hulk Landing/Hero Landing/Hell Dive, and Juggernaut are all deferred to a dedicated tier/equipment balancing pass.

## Gear Worx Stations

**Status:** Fully decided at the mechanic level — three blocks (Dock, Workbench, Growth Chamber), their construction recipes, and their shared-pool networking are all confirmed. Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-gear-worx-notes.md`**.

**Definition:** The suit/gadget maintenance-and-progression counterpart to the FL — reuses the FL's Floor/Knitting network (a connected 5×5 Floor footprint, no separate controller block) rather than inventing new infrastructure. Any 2 of the 3 stations on a connected footprint merge into a shared resource pool + tabbed GUI; the third can join later.

**Known stations:**
- **Dock** — the suit's rack. Handles Grafting/Amputation (equipping/removing a suit — "Field Amputation" costs damage, "Assisted Amputation" costs time instead, per the Suits removal formula above), add-on Frame slotting, passive refuel/repair, a player-heal bonus while docked and standing still, the suit point-spend menu, and suit respec (resets to the last tier's baseline, timed at 1 second per point currently spent).
- **Workbench** — the gadget maintenance hub: internal gadget storage (starts at 9 slots, doubling per tier), passive recharge for fuel-based gadgets, general-purpose vanilla-style durability repair for any `Damageable` item, and Add-on Frame loading/exchangeable-parts swapping. Fabrication itself still routes through the FL.
- **Growth Chamber** — handles all tier-ups (suit and gadget), via a timed, fluid-only "printed onto the existing item" process reusing the FL's printing-recipe method.

**Construction:** Each station is a two-block (bottom/top) implant build, following the vanilla-door "paired blockstates" precedent — each half has its own independent implant recipe, and only the bottom half's recipe requires a Brain Block (one Brain per station total, matching the FL Core's own control-block cost).

**Naming glossary:** Grafting/Grafted (suit equip/worn state), Field Amputation (rushed removal), Assisted Amputation (Dock-based removal).

**Open questions:** Full recipe/material list for Growth Chamber's suit tier-ups (gadget tier-ups are already fluid-only, per-gadget). Whether the Tablet's point-spend access ever grows into a broader equipment-management hub.

## Tools

**Status:** Category defined; six tools named. Syringe, Flask, and Beaker have their own companion-doc detail; **Scalpel, Suture Kit, and Forceps now also have confirmed functions**, all tied to a shared mechanism — the Inert Tumor block chain — detailed in the new companion doc: **`dermicraft-tools-notes.md`**.

**Definition:** Simple, hand-held implements — direct-use instruments rather than task-automating devices (that's Gadgets/Machines). Surgical/medical theme fits the mad-science lean naturally.

**Known tools:**
- **Scalpel** — cuts living tumor blocks to harvest items (Inert Tumor or any mutation), and cuts stitches to reopen a Stitched Tumor. Secondary player-facing use: Bloodletting, a Poison cure with its own bleed/debuff cost. Full detail in `dermicraft-tools-notes.md`.
- **Suture Kit** — stitches a Marred Tumor closed, or sutures an item into one as part of forcing a mutation/healing recipe. Secondary player-facing use: the Sutured status (accelerated healing, with a harsh forced-removal penalty). Full detail in `dermicraft-tools-notes.md`.
- **Forceps** — the tool for recovering the mod's "living" blocks. Breaking such a block the normal way **destroys** it (you get nothing back but its contents), so the Forceps is the only way to get the block itself back — picking it up still drops its contents on the ground like a break, but additionally hands you the block. No crouch required. Confirmed to have no secondary use. Full detail in `dermicraft-tools-notes.md`.

### Syringes

**Status:** Variant logic, naming convention, primary use, and now **two** confirmed secondary uses.

**What it is:** A Syringe holds a fluid; the specific fluid it's filled with is what defines each variant, named **"[Fluid] Syringe"** (e.g. Crude Slurry Syringe) — a distinct naming pattern from Flask's "Flask of [Fluid]." **Primary use confirmed:** draws fluid from certain blocks (filtering mechanism still TBD) and injects it into a Stitched Tumor to complete a recipe — this is the actual mechanism behind crafting Machines by hand, resolving what was previously an open question. **Secondary use confirmed:** the Crude Slurry Syringe forces instant accelerated biological effects on passive mobs (wool regrowth, mob maturation, egg-laying, milk refresh, and now Squid ink release without killing it). **Second secondary use confirmed:** a C-Stuff Syringe injected into a Creeper disrupts its explosion trigger for a few seconds without making it inert — it still swells/hisses, just can't detonate — repeatable with a cooldown; the first Syringe secondary use aimed at a hostile mob rather than a passive one. Full detail in `dermicraft-tools-notes.md`.

**Capacity:** `100 mB` (millibuckets).

**Open questions:** Fluid-draw filtering mechanism. Full roster of valid draw-source blocks. Exact numeric values for the Squid and Creeper effects. Whether Syringe variants beyond Crude Slurry and C-Stuff have further secondary uses yet to be worked out.

Also has other uses beyond transport — to be worked out, full reasoning living in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-flask-notes.md`**.

## Fluids

Dermicraft's fluids split into three families so far: **Catalysts** (process/transformation fluids), **Slurries** (the mod's fuel fluids, though individual slurries can have secondary uses — e.g. as a Catalyst base), and **Crafting** fluids (fluids whose main purpose is as recipe ingredients). Each gets its own heading below since they serve different roles.

### Catalysts

Not a tiered progression — each has a distinct purpose, though some use others as a base ingredient (e.g. Evolution Catalyst uses a Primitive Catalyst pool).

Known catalysts:
- **Primitive Catalyst** — first catalyst, simple/early-game recipe. Fully decided (recipe, color, fluid properties). Name doesn't describe its function — it's the only catalyst named for *when/how simple* rather than *what it does*.
- **Evolution Catalyst** — final step in a machine's forced-evolution upgrade. Recipe and color decided. Marks the player's transition toward Stage 2. Now has a **second, late-game recipe** substituting Living Glowstone for raw Glowstone Dust, producing the same result more efficiently — the original recipe is unchanged and still serves as the Stage 1→2 gate. (Flagged: this second recipe's "late-Stage-2" label may need revisiting now that Living Glowstone itself is a Stage 3 fluid — see `dermicraft-catalyst-notes.md`.)
- **Living Catalyst** (renamed from "Life Catalyst") — core mechanic and recipe now defined: **Dragon's Milk + Molten Quartz + Blaze Essence**, combined in an upgraded Effluentcer, then applied via the **Gestator** to turn a non-living fluid into its living, self-replicating counterpart (e.g. Molten Glowstone → Living Glowstone, Molten Netherite → Living Netherite, plain Metal Blends → Living Iron/Copper/Gold). Confirmed to handle **Mild Radiation** hazard-handling for later Stage 2 machines (toughness + self-repair, not blocking-style shielding). Introduced in a deliberately difficult, low-yield form at the Stage 2/3 boundary rather than waiting for a clean Tier 3/4 unlock — Stage 3 is now defined around scaling this up to real production.
- **Ascendant Catalyst** — confirmed as the mod's top-tier, "god tier" endgame equipment catalyst. Hazard classification: `Hazardous → Radiation → Severe`. Deliberately difficult to handle — no confirmed handling solution yet. Ingredients, recipe, color, and exact Tier/Stage placement still open.

**Living Glowstone (not itself a Catalyst):** A fluid logged alongside the Catalyst family for proximity — it's Evolution Catalyst's late-game alternate ingredient and Living Catalyst's flagship product. **Stage 3** (moved from "late Stage 2" — it can't exist before Living Catalyst, which is itself a Stage 2/3-boundary product). Recipe: **Molten Glowstone + Living Catalyst → Living Glowstone**, via the Gestator; Molten Glowstone (Glowstone Dust + Lava, Masticator) is its plain Stage 2 precursor. Notable as the mod's first genuinely emissive fluid (matches vanilla Glowstone block's light level) and its first self-replicating ("living") fluid, with a deliberately negative density (flows upward — the mod's first fluid to use that property). Hazard classification: `Hazardous → Radiation → Mild`. Self-replication is intended to be slow by default, with boosts planned for later. Full detail in `dermicraft-catalyst-notes.md`.

Full reasoning, ingredient lists, color decisions, and fluid properties for the catalysts worked out so far live in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-catalyst-notes.md`**.

### Slurries

**Status:** Family purpose defined (fuel); Main Line order and progression rule established; full detail moved to its own companion doc.

The mod's fuel family. Slurries have a **Main Line** progression — Crude → Concentrated → Refined → Enriched → Superior — where each step up increases speed (work per machine process cycle) and decreases use rate (fuel consumed per cycle) relative to the one before it. Only Crude's exact values are set so far.

A second, independent fuel family — **Serums** — has since been scoped. Where the Main Line is built around sustained efficiency (more work per cycle, less fuel consumed, the better the slurry), Serums are built around a fast, high-impact burst with a real cost attached. They share the same three-stat model as Slurries (speed/use rate/heal modifiers) but push values to extremes or invert them — Serums are the first fuel type to use a negative heal modifier, repurposing it as damage-over-time. Their defining trait is **unconditional burn**: once loaded, a Serum burns down regardless of whether the machine is processing, idle, or has nothing queued — unlike Slurries, which only draw down while actively processing. Two members confirmed so far: **Healing Serum** (large HP boost, but forces speed to flat zero while active) and **Booster Serum** (large speed boost, at the cost of ongoing HP damage). Targeted for Stage 2 introduction. Full detail in `dermicraft-slurry-notes.md`.

Full property model, Crude's baseline values, and per-slurry status for the rest of the line live in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-slurry-notes.md`**.

**Open question:** Does "Main Line" imply branch/side lines later, or is this the complete roster? Confirmed as a live possibility — an **experimental branch** of slurries may exist alongside it: slurries that don't fit the Main Line's derived-from-baseline progression (i.e. not just another speed-up/use-rate-down step). Concept not yet defined beyond that. A dedicated session is planned to work this out, and some of the Main Line content above may get reworked as a result — treat it as the latest settled state, not necessarily final.

### Crafting

**Status:** Family expanded this session — nine fluids now decided across three sub-groups (the original six Blends, plus a new Sediment Blends sub-group), plus a new alternate route into Ferrous Blend.

**Definition:** Fluids whose main purpose is as recipe ingredients, rather than fuel (Slurries) or a process/transformation step (Catalysts). Made by the **Masticator** (see Machines above).

**Base fluid — no single rule.** Carbon Blend, Calcium Blend, and Protein Blend each use **Water**. **Ferrous Blend, Cuprous Blend, and Aurous Blend use Primitive Catalyst instead** — chosen specifically to avoid the "metal rusting in water" visual implication a clear-fluid base would carry. The newer Sediment Blends (Stone/Silica/Clay) use **Water**. Don't assume water as a default for any future Crafting fluid; check case by case.

**Known Crafting fluids:**
- **Carbon Blend** — corresponds to Primitive Catalyst's charcoal/coal component.
- **Calcium Blend** — corresponds to Primitive Catalyst's bone component.
- **Protein Blend** — corresponds to Primitive Catalyst's raw-meat component. Made from any `MEAT_FOOD`-tagged item (food-based yield). Also feeds a second, distinct chain: Protein Blend + Iron Nugget (via the Metastasizer) → Blood Nugget → (via the Masticator, + Primitive Catalyst) → Ferrous Blend, a deliberately low-yield "trace iron from blood" route.
- **Ferrous Blend, Cuprous Blend, Aurous Blend** — correspond to Iron, Copper, and Gold respectively. Use Primitive Catalyst as their base (not water), and have **no pre-Masticator manual recipe**.
- **Stone Blend, Silica Blend, Clay Blend** (the "Sediment Blends") — three water-based Crafting fluids covering the overworld stone, sand, and clay families respectively. Each is both a Masticator recipe (block + water → fluid) and a Metastasizer duplication fluid (fluid + an existing block on that fluid's whitelist → duplicate of that block, recipe-by-recipe). Cross-feed into each other for boosted yields (Silica Blend acting as the hub between Stone and Clay), support recycling of fired/crafted derivatives (Glass, Bricks, Terracotta, etc. — Mud Bricks excluded), and double as environmental hazards when pooled, each modeled on a different real trapping hazard (Stone = wet concrete, Clay = bog mud, Silica = quicksand). Deliberately designed to allow infinite, repeatable supply — not a concern since the source blocks are cheap and common, the same philosophy behind vanilla's infinite cobblestone generator. Full detail in `dermicraft-crafting-notes.md`.

**Purpose — partial nuance:** Carbon/Calcium/Protein Blend fit the original framing of providing a **more efficient alternate route** to a fluid that already has an established recipe (e.g. Primitive Catalyst via puddle vs. via Blends). The Metal Blends and the Sediment Blends don't — neither has a pre-existing recipe to shortcut; they're Masticator-native, and for the Sediment Blends specifically, the point is a sustainable supply loop rather than efficiency. **Molten Redstone** (renamed from "Liquid Redstone") is the first Stage 2 Crafting Blend and the first example of the Stage 2 → lava-based rule.

**Stage 2 Crafting Blends use Lava as their base fluid** — a deliberate break from Stage 1's Water/Primitive Catalyst bases — and make up the **Molten family**: Molten Redstone, Molten Quartz, Molten Glowstone, Molten Amethyst, Molten Diamond, Molten Lapis, and the Molten Raw Netherite Scrap → Molten Netherite chain, plus creature-drop "Essences" (Blaze, Ghast, Wither, Ender — no "Molten" prefix) and the standalone Molten Soul Silica. The family exists specifically to gate **rare/hard-to-liquefy** materials behind Stage 2 — deliberately **not** extended to the already-ungated Metal Blends (Ferrous/Cuprous/Aurous). Ender Essence is notable as **Metaphysical Severe** hazard-tagged (alongside its inherited Extreme Heat) — see Hazard tag hierarchy above and `dermicraft-hazard-effects-notes.md`. **Molten Obsidian was cut** — replaced by a real Water + Lava → Obsidian recipe on the new **Tier 2 Render Kiln** (see `dermicraft-machine-notes.md` → Render Kiln), which also hosts a handful of other two-fluid recipes (quenched Redstone Block/Netherite Ingot, Netherrack from Protein Blend + Stone Blend). See `dermicraft-crafting-notes.md` for full detail.

**All Metal — the mod's first confirmed Stage 4 fluid.** Unifies all four Living Metals (Living Iron/Copper/Gold/Netherite) into one fluid via a further-evolved (4-input) Effluentcer. Dual hazard-tagged — `Hazardous → Radiation → Severe` **and** `Hazardous → Biohazard` — the first fluid to carry two hazard classifications at once, and the first confirmed use of the **Biohazard** tag (Dragon's Milk, above, is the second). Intended as both a top-tier crafting ingredient (Ascendant Catalyst and other god-tier gear) and a material in its own right. Full detail in `dermicraft-crafting-notes.md`.

Full reasoning, exact item lists, yields, and open questions for all of the above live in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-crafting-notes.md`**.

### Liquid Foods

**Status:** System model and the first three edible fluids decided; per-fluid secondary effects deferred (hook exists, unapplied). Full detail in the companion doc: **`dermicraft-liquid-foods-notes.md`**.

**What it is:** Treats certain fluids as drinkable food via the **Feeder Bladder** (see Tools → Bladder above). Mental model: vanilla food plus a flat mB tax per bite — hunger/saturation and mB-per-drink cost are deliberately decoupled (no fluid-to-hunger conversion constant tying them together, unlike the Fuel Bladder's fuel-side conversion rate). Governed by a new `edible_fluid` data map (keyed by source fluid id) — a fluid absent from the map simply isn't drinkable. Out of scope for v1: per-fluid secondary effects, healing, the suit-integrated Feeder Bladder add-on's own scaling, and grade/Heal-modifier scaling.

## Working conventions established so far

These are the patterns used when designing Dermicraft content — worth continuing in future chats for consistency:

- **Colors and effects start from ingredient/mechanic logic, not random pick — but logic is the baseline, not a cage.** Work out what the real ingredients/mechanics would actually produce, then it's fair game to deliberately push the result toward a design goal (more vibrant, more "mad science," etc.) as a conscious next step — same as Evolution Catalyst's color being pushed more vibrant on request after the literal-mix baseline was established. Reasoned first, then styled — vibes aren't excluded, they just come second.
- **Tone target going forward:** bio-horror with a mad-science lean — experimentation, forced/unnatural change, lab-process feel — rather than straightforward gore or visceral horror.
- **Fluid physics (viscosity, density, temperature, motionScale — NeoForge `FluidType`) are also reasoned about, not just assigned.** Vanilla water and lava values serve as reference anchors. Intentional inversions of normal relationships (e.g. a thick fluid with an unusually responsive motion scale, or a fluid with negative density that flows upward) can be used deliberately as a subtle "wrongness" detail fitting the bio-horror theme — these get flagged when spotted, but the final call belongs to whoever's running the project.
- **Per-item logs capture reasoning, not just final values:** decision, why, alternates considered and why they were dropped, open questions. This avoids having to re-derive logic later or accidentally contradict an earlier decision. A category spins off its own companion notes file once it accumulates enough real content to warrant it — a judgment call, not an automatic rule per category. Every category (Catalysts, Slurries, Flask, Beaker, Gadgets, Suits, Gear Worx Stations, Machines, Tools, and Crafting fluids) has now done so; individual suit-signature weapons (One Punch, Scout Blade) have gone a level further and split off their own dedicated breakdown docs beyond the shared Suit notes file.
- **Catalyst naming is mostly function-first** (Evolution Catalyst evolves things), with Primitive Catalyst as the deliberate exception.
- **Water is the base fluid for *some* Slurries and Crafting fluids — not a family-wide rule.** Crude Slurry, three of the original Crafting Blends (Carbon, Calcium, Protein), and the newer Sediment Blends (Stone, Silica, Clay) happen to use water — that's just true of those, not a law the whole family follows. Ferrous/Cuprous/Aurous Blend (the Metal Blends) use Primitive Catalyst instead, picked specifically to avoid the "metal rusting in water" visual implication a clear-fluid base would carry. **Catalysts tend to chain off other established fluids instead:** Primitive Catalyst's base is Crude Slurry, and Evolution Catalyst's base is Primitive Catalyst (not water, even indirectly-related water-based fluids don't make a Catalyst "water-based"). Don't assume water as a default for any new fluid without checking that fluid's own family/precedent first.
- **Every ingredient, item, or fluid should have at least one use beyond being a single-purpose recipe ingredient — but this should never be forced.** A forcing function against dead-end items that only exist to feed one recipe with no other reason to exist — e.g. F-Stuff isn't just an ingredient toward Primitive Catalyst, it's also directly cookable into MRE. The extra use has to fit naturally from the item's own identity/logic, same as everything else in this list (colors, effects, etc. are reasoned from logic first) — if no sensible second use presents itself, the item stays single-purpose rather than getting an arbitrary use tacked on just to satisfy the rule.
- **Prefer Dermicraft-native processes over vanilla crafting.** A core mod goal is moving away from vanilla crafting methods in favor of Dermicraft's own machines/processes (Masticator, Effluentcer, Metastasizer, etc.). Vanilla crafting/cooking stays acceptable as an occasional exception — not eliminated entirely — particularly where it fills a necessary gap with no Dermicraft-native alternative yet (e.g. furnace/smoker cooking as the required bootstrap step for a player's first MRE, before the Metastasizer's duplication loop can take over). Existing vanilla-adjacent recipes like Beaker's 3 Glass Blocks or Carbon Blend's Coal Block input fit this same "minor, necessary exception" pattern rather than needing rework.
- **Resolving conflicts with a parallel/alternate recipe.** When a new ingredient, fluid, or mechanic creates a timing/logic conflict with an existing recipe (e.g. an ingredient that wouldn't be available until after the recipe is supposed to unlock it), adding a second, parallel recipe is a valid fix — not just replacing or blocking the original. Example: Evolution Catalyst keeps its original Stage-1-reachable recipe (unchanged, still the Stage 1→2 gate) alongside a new late-Stage-2 recipe using Living Glowstone.
- **The "hand-in-a-bucket" hazard test.** A rule of thumb for judging whether a fluid should be Tier-hazardous (restricted from Tier 1 tools/Gadgets/Machines, same as lava): if a person could stick a hand directly into a bucket of the fluid and nothing traumatic happens, it's not considered hazardous for machine-handling purposes — even if the fluid is dangerous in bulk/immersion (e.g. Silica Blend's quicksand-style drowning risk when pooled deep enough to submerge a person). The hazard classification is about the fluid's substance/chemistry, not about volume-based environmental danger.
- **A machine's signature mechanic is a default, not a hard rule.** Established with the Metastasizer: its core behavior ("fluid consumed, item is a non-consumed pattern, output is a duplicate of that item") is the primary/intended use case, but individual recipes can define a different output entirely without breaking anything (e.g. Protein Blend + Iron Nugget → Blood Nugget, not a copy of the Nugget). Applies to other machines too — a machine's defining function describes its main purpose, not an absolute constraint on every recipe it can run.
- **Repeatable/compounding escalation is acceptable for cheap, common resources.** Infinite supply loops are fine as long as they aren't *easy* relative to the value of what's being produced — time/effort is an acceptable limiter on its own, the same way vanilla's infinite cobblestone generator works. This is judged per-resource: cheap overworld blocks (Sediment Blends) can compound freely, while effort-gated resources (iron, via the Blood Nugget → Ferrous Blend chain) get deliberately low yields to keep the loop "low but fair" rather than a real substitute for the existing effort-based systems (mining, Silk Touch, etc.).
- **Mechanisms fail safely by refusing invalid input outright, rather than processing and failing partway.** Established with Drinker (won't attempt to draw a hazardous fluid at all) and Masticator (won't process a recipe whose output exceeds the buffer), and now believed to extend to the Marred Tumor (refuses to be sutured shut if its contents don't match a valid recipe — pending confirmation). A recurring signature of how Dermicraft's mechanics behave: blocking bad input before it happens, not rolling dice on whether it succeeds.
- **Player-facing risk and machine-facing safety follow two different patterns, deliberately.** Player mechanics (Suture Kit's Sutured status, Scalpel's Bloodletting) can carry a self-inflicted, fully foreseeable cost — the benefit is guaranteed if the player doesn't make a mistake (sprinting, taking damage, etc.), but failure is sharply punished when they do. This is "high benefit, high cost upon failure," not "high risk, high reward" — no dice are rolled; the player chooses whether to slip. Machines and Gadgets, by contrast, default to the fail-safe refusal pattern above for now, with harsher machine-side failure consequences flagged as a possible later addition once core systems are stable.
- **Bulk item storage must respect vanilla's 99-count save cap.** A single Minecraft `ItemStack` can render and network-sync with a count well above 64, but its **NBT/data codec hard-caps `count` at 99** — saving an over-99 stack (block-entity save, chunk save, or a dropped item entity) crashes with `Value must be within range [1;99]`. So any block holding more than one stack of an item in a single slot (the Craw holds 10 stacks / 640) has to (a) serialize its own storage manually — persist the item type from a count-1 copy plus the real amount as a separate integer, not via the default handler serialization — and (b) split its drops into ≤64-count pieces on break. Automation and GUI interactions are already safe (extraction and vanilla clicks move ≤64 at a time). This applies to the Craw now and to any future bulk-storage block (its planned capacity-scaling, automation-hub storage, etc.).
- **Prefer multiplicative growth over additive, for numeric progression, wherever possible.** When a value scales across tiers/steps (capacity, output, cost, etc.), default to scaling it by a **multiplier** rather than adding a flat amount each step — confirmed while discussing Chitin Tank's capacity relative to Skin Tank's. Exact multiplier is chosen per-case (a flat 2× isn't a hard rule, just the instinct to reach for first) and can be tuned down if straight doubling feels too aggressive too early.
- **Dip Crafting — fluid-handler block as a crafting surface.** Holding a Stick and right-clicking any fluid-handler block currently holding a compatible fluid converts the Stick into a crafted item on the spot, bypassing the crafting table. Uses a custom recipe type. Current confirmed recipes: **Carbon Blend → Torches** (1 Stick + 75 mB → 4 Torches; 18.75 mB/torch vs. vanilla's cheapest 27.5 mB-equivalent/torch); **Molten Redstone → Redstone Torches** (1 Stick + 75 mB → 1 Redstone Torch; vs. vanilla's 1 Dust = 110 mB-equivalent). Both support stack-dipping: a held stack of Sticks is processed in one interaction. Partial fill applies at the batch level — as many full dips as the fluid covers, stopping cleanly rather than refusing the whole batch. Any crafted items that don't fit the player's inventory drop on the ground. Not committed as a universal pattern — more recipes may be added when they feel natural, but it's not a forcing function.
